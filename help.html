Lyricpad Help

Lyricpad is a lyric editor that counts syllables as you type and helps you find rhymes, synonyms and fresh lines. Here's how each part works.

Quick start

  1. Give your song a title (top of the editor) and start writing. Each line shows its syllable count on the left.
  2. Need a rhyme? Double-click the word in your lyrics, or type it into the Rhymes search box.
  3. Need a better word? Open the Thesaurus tab and double-click a word in your lyrics.
  4. Stuck on a line? The Suggest lines tab asks an AI for ideas (needs your own API key).
  5. Press Save to keep the song in your Google Drive, or Export .txt to download it.
On a phone the editor is at the top and the Rhymes / Thesaurus / Suggest lines tabs are below it. Tip: add Lyricpad to your home screen from your browser's share menu so it opens like an app.

Syllable counts

Every line gets a live count in the left margin. The line you're on is highlighted, and the bar under the editor shows its number and count.

[Chorus] 10I packed the car before the sun came up 6–7The fire burned out tonight 4?Skrrting past the lights
  • Ranges like 6–7 mean a word can be sung either way, e.g. fire (1 or 2) or every (2 or 3). Pick whichever fits your melody.
  • A “?” means a word isn't in the dictionary, so its count is an estimate.
  • [Square brackets] are for section tags like [Verse 1], [Chorus] or [x2]. They're never counted, wherever they appear in a line.
  • On a computer, hover over a count to see how each word was counted.
  • Lyric spellings like runnin', goin' and 'cause are understood.

Rhymes

Look up a word in any of these ways:

  • Type a word, or a short phrase like phone me, into the search box on the Rhymes tab.
  • Double-click a word in your lyrics.
  • On a computer, put the cursor on a word and press Alt+R or the Rhyme this word button. On a blank line, this rhymes the last word of the line above, which is handy right after you press Enter.

Choose which kinds of rhyme you want:

OptionWhat it findsExample
PerfectThe same sounds from the stressed vowel to the endheart → start, apart
NearSlant rhymes: close, but not exactheart → hard, dark; time → mine
Vowel matchSame vowel sounds, any consonantslove → jump

Results are grouped by syllable count. Use the syllables filter to show only one length, and tick Rare words to include unusual ones. Tap a result to copy it.

Thesaurus

Find a word that means the right thing, and optionally lands the rhyme too.

  • Open the Thesaurus tab, then double-click a word in your lyrics (or press Alt+T, or tap Use selected word). You can also just type a word.
  • Synonyms are grouped by meaning, with a short definition for each, followed by a longer list of related words. Small numbers show each word's syllables.
  • Tap a synonym to swap it into your lyrics in place of the word you looked up. Capital letters are kept, and Ctrl/⌘+Z undoes it.
  • Fill in Must rhyme with to see only synonyms that rhyme with another word. For example, synonyms of sad that rhyme with dawn include withdrawn and forlorn. Near rhymes are shown in italics. Words under further afield are synonyms of synonyms, so check the meaning still fits.

Suggest lines (AI)

Ask an AI for ideas for a line. It's optional, and it uses your own API key.

One-time setup

  1. Open the Suggest lines tab and expand AI settings.
  2. Choose a provider and paste a key:
    • Gemini (Google): free key at aistudio.google.com. The free tier has rate limits.
    • Claude (Anthropic): key at console.anthropic.com. Paid per use, roughly 0.05¢–2¢ per request depending on the model.
  3. Pick a model. Your key stays in your browser and is only sent to the provider you chose.

Getting suggestions

  1. Click a line in your lyrics. It appears under Selected line.
  2. Optionally describe the Topic / feel, e.g. leaving a small town, bittersweet.
  3. Choose Rewrite this line (alternatives for it) or Write the next line (what could come after it).
  4. Line starts with / Line ends with (optional): type the words the new line must begin or end with, e.g. I never knew or in the morning light, and the AI completes the rest. You can use either or both. Typing in the box ticks it; untick to ignore it. If a suggestion doesn't keep to your words, it's marked with a ⚠. When the ending is fixed, the rhyme is skipped.
  5. Syllables per line starts at the selected line's count. Type a number or use − / + to change it, or untick it for no limit.
  6. Rhyme: suggestions rhyme by default with the last word of the selected line (on a blank line, the nearest line above that has words). The caption under Override the selected rhyme with always shows the word in use and changes as you click other lines. To use a different word, tick the box and type it; tick it and leave it blank for no rhyme at all. Untick it to go back to the default.
  7. In “Rewrite this line” mode, Try to reword this line to fit the rhyme keeps the line's meaning and changes only its wording so it ends on a different word that rhymes with the rhyme word, with no new ideas. It's greyed out while the Topic / feel box has text (clear it to use this), and when the line is empty, there's no rhyme word, or Line ends with is on.
  8. Press Suggest lines. Each idea shows its real syllable count, highlighted when it hits your number. Use Replace line, Insert below or Copy.
Fast mode (on by default) makes the AI answer much quicker by thinking less. Untick it when you want more considered lines and don't mind waiting.

Writing a whole song from a structure

  1. In Suno Style Creator, generate a song and press Copy on its Song Structure.
  2. In the Suggest lines tab, fill in Topic / feel: it's what the song will be about.
  3. Expand Write a whole song from a structure and paste the structure into the box. Underneath it shows how many sections and lines it found. The box remembers what you pasted.
  4. Press Write lyrics. The AI writes every sung section with its number of lines, each line's syllable count and the rhyme scheme; instrumental sections keep just their tag, like [INTRO: fuzz guitar]. A section with the same name as an earlier one (a second [CHORUS]) repeats it.
  5. Each line shows its real syllable count, highlighted when it hits the target; a miss shows count/target, e.g. 7/8. Use Replace song (Ctrl/Cmd+Z undoes), Add to end or Copy, then polish lines with the rest of the tab.
A whole song is a bigger job than a few lines: it can take a minute, and with Fast mode off the syllable counts are usually closer.

Saving songs

  • Automatically in your browser: the song you're working on is kept on this device, so closing the tab won't lose it. It isn't shared between devices, though.
  • Save (or Ctrl/⌘+S) stores the song in a Lyricpad folder in your Google Drive. The first time, you sign in with Google and allow access. Lyricpad can only see files it creates, nothing else in your Drive. After that, changes save automatically a few seconds after you stop typing, and the bar under the editor shows “Saved to Drive”.
  • Staying connected: Google gives Lyricpad one hour of Drive access at a time, and it lasts even if you close the tab. When the hour is nearly up, the next time you tap a button Lyricpad asks Google for another hour. You may see Google's window open and close by itself. If you've been away longer than that, auto-save pauses (the bar says so) until you tap any button or press Save. Typing never opens Google's window.
  • Songs lists your saved songs to open on any device, and starts a New song.
  • Export .txt downloads the song as a text file.
  • Copy ▾ puts the song on your clipboard, ready to paste anywhere. Choose Copy lyrics for the text exactly as written, or Copy without [tags] to remove everything in square brackets, like [Verse 1], [Chorus] or [x2]. Lines that held only a tag are dropped, and blank lines between sections are kept. Tick Include the song title at the top to add the title (Lyricpad remembers this choice). Your lyrics in the editor are never changed.
  • If you edit the same song on two devices, Lyricpad notices, stops auto-saving, and asks before overwriting the other version.

Questions

A syllable count looks wrong.

Counts come from a pronunciation dictionary, so they match how a word is usually spoken. Singers often stretch or squeeze words, and ranges like 6–7 show the common alternatives. A “?” means the word was guessed from its spelling.

The page looks broken or out of date after an update.

Reload the page. If that doesn't help, do a hard refresh: Ctrl+Shift+R, or ⌘+Shift+R on a Mac. On a phone, close the tab and open it again.

Google says the app isn't verified.

That can appear until Google finishes checking the app. It's safe to continue: Lyricpad only gets access to the song files it creates in your Drive.

Can I change the colours?

Use the Theme button at the top right: Light, Dark, or Auto (follows your device).
